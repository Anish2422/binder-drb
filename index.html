    Confidentiality and AI use

    Board members are bound to keep project information confidential, and the DRBF's guidance on AI asks Boards to protect confidentiality, review every output, keep decisions their own, and be open with the parties about how AI was used. Binder is built around those four points.

    How case documents are handled

    • Each case is a separate private workspace. Documents are not shared across cases or users.
    • Documents are processed only under terms that prohibit using them to train any model.
    • The Board can delete a case and all of its documents at any time.
    • Nothing is sent to a public chat service.

    What Binder does and doesn't do

    • It checks statements and citations against the record, builds the chronology, suggests questions, and checks the draft for consistency.
    • It does not evaluate entitlement, predict an outcome, or draft findings, analysis or recommendations.

    Disclosure to the parties

    A plain statement the Board can include in its report or read at the meeting, if it chooses to disclose:

    The Board used a document review tool to check citations and factual statements in the parties' submissions against the record, to assemble a chronology, and to check the consistency of terms and dates in its draft. The tool did not evaluate the merits. All findings, analysis and recommendations are the Board's own.
